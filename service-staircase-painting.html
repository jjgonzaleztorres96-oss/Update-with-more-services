Staircase Painting in Toronto — Durability Meets Precision

A staircase is one of the most demanding painted surfaces in any home. The railing is gripped every time someone goes up or down. The balusters are hit and bumped regularly. The risers and stringers take scuffs from shoes. Standard wall paint applied to these surfaces will show wear within months — not because it was applied poorly, but because it was the wrong product for a high-contact, high-abrasion surface.

Cornerstone Pro Painting uses alkyd-hybrid and waterborne alkyd enamel products on staircase and railing surfaces — products that cure harder than standard latex, resist grip marks and finger oils, and maintain their appearance under sustained daily contact. We have painted staircases and railings across Toronto and the GTA in wood, metal, and composite, and we know what each material requires.

Our Process — Step by Step

Step 1: Surface Assessment — We evaluate the existing finish condition, substrate material (wood, metal, MDF), and identify any areas that need repair before painting.

Step 2: Preparation — Surfaces sanded to create adhesion profile, existing paint condition assessed, glossy areas deglossed, bare areas primed with appropriate primer.

Step 3: Railing & Baluster Work — Railing and balusters are painted systematically — avoiding wet surfaces that would be gripped — with a hard-curing enamel product.

Step 4: Risers & Stringers — Risers and stringers painted in a durable semi-gloss or gloss finish, working in alternating sections to maintain stair access.

Step 5: Stairwell Walls — Adjacent stairwell walls painted in the colour and sheen appropriate to the rest of the home's palette.

Why Toronto Homeowners Choose Cornerstone Pro Painting

Staircase projects require sequencing — we can't take the stairs out of service entirely while paint cures, so we work in sections to maintain access throughout the project. Our crew plans this phasing before the first brush goes down, and homeowners always have safe stair access throughout. We also advise on curing timelines so the new finish is not stressed by heavy traffic before it has fully hardened.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use the stairs while you are painting?

Yes. We work in alternating sections so there is always a safe path. We schedule the final coat for a time when we can have a few hours of reduced traffic for the fresh surface to set.

What paint do you use on railings?

We use alkyd-hybrid or waterborne alkyd enamel — products that cure significantly harder than standard latex and hold up to the grip and contact wear that railings endure. Benjamin Moore Advance and Sherwin-Williams Emerald Urethane are two products we rely on for this application.

Can you paint metal railings?

Yes. Metal railings are cleaned, lightly sanded, primed with a metal-appropriate primer, and finished with an exterior-grade enamel for maximum durability.

What sheen is best for staircase surfaces?

Semi-gloss at minimum for railings and balusters — gloss preferred for maximum durability and ease of cleaning. Risers and stringers in semi-gloss. Stairwell walls in eggshell or satin.

What does staircase painting cost in Toronto?

A standard single staircase with railing and balusters runs $800–$1,800 depending on the number of balusters, the material, and whether the stairwell walls are included. Written quote after walkthrough.

Can you refinish bare or stripped wood treads?

Bare wood treads are a separate scope from painting — they are typically stained and finished rather than painted, using a floor-grade product. We assess treads during the estimate and recommend the appropriate treatment.
