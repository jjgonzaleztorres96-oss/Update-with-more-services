Painting Your Toronto Condo: What Every Owner Should Know First

Painting a condo in Toronto is not like painting a house. Building rules, elevator booking, neighbour considerations, and product restrictions all come into play. After completing dozens of condo paint jobs across the city, here's what I tell owners before we start.

Jay Gonzalez · April 2025 · 6 min read

Check With Your Property Management First

Before booking any interior painting work in a Toronto condo, contact your building's property management office. Most condo corporations have specific rules about when tradespeople can work (noise restrictions typically apply from 8am to 5pm or 9am to 6pm), how they must access the building (using a service elevator or freight entrance), and what products can be used in units connected to shared HVAC systems.

Some buildings require a work permit or advance notice for any interior renovation work, including painting. Failing to follow these rules can result in your contractor being turned away, fines, or conflicts with your neighbours that are entirely avoidable with a five-minute call to management in advance.

Elevator Booking and Access Planning

Moving painting supplies into a high-rise — ladders, roller frames, paint cans, drop cloths — requires elevator access. Most condo buildings require the freight elevator to be booked in advance for any trade work that involves moving materials. This booking typically has to be coordinated at least 48–72 hours ahead, and available windows are limited.

A professional painting crew that works regularly in Toronto condos will know to plan for this. When you call us for a condo estimate, one of the first questions we ask is what the building's elevator booking process looks like, and we factor that into our scheduling. An unprepared crew showing up with gear and no elevator booking wastes everyone's time.

Low-VOC Requirements and Ventilation

Shared ventilation systems in high-rise buildings make VOC emissions a concern that goes beyond your own unit. Many newer condo buildings explicitly require low-VOC or zero-VOC products for any interior work — both as a courtesy to neighbours and because high-VOC products introduce compounds into shared air systems that other residents breathe.

Cornerstone Pro Painting uses low-VOC and zero-VOC interior paints as our standard on all residential projects, which means condo work aligns naturally with our product selection. We've never been turned away from a Toronto condo for product compliance, and we're happy to provide product data sheets to building management on request.

Protecting the Unit and Common Areas

Condo floors, especially hardwood and laminate, are exactly the kind of surfaces that paint drips destroy. We protect all floors with heavy canvas drop cloths — not plastic sheeting, which moves and is slippery — and we take extra care at the unit entrance where paint cans are moved in and out. We also protect any hallway carpet or tile outside the unit during move-in and move-out.

Balcony railings and surfaces should not receive paint overspray that could affect neighbouring units or common areas. We mask balcony railings and furniture carefully, and we never spray paint on a high-rise balcony where wind drift is unpredictable and uncontrollable.

What Condo Painting Projects Typically Cost

A one-bedroom or one-plus-den condo in Toronto — typically 550–750 sq ft — runs $1,400–$2,200 for a full interior repaint including walls, trim, doors, and ceiling. Two-bedroom units at 850–1,100 sq ft run $2,000–$3,200. These numbers assume standard ceiling heights and no significant surface repair work. Builder white to a new colour is a similar price to a same-colour refresh — both require similar preparation.
