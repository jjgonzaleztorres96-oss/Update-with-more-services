Garage Floor Epoxy Coating in the GTA: What to Expect

Garage floor epoxy has become one of our most requested services over the past few years. A grey concrete slab becomes a clean, glossy, easy-to-maintain surface that makes the whole garage feel like a different space. Here's what the process actually looks like from start to finish.

Jay Gonzalez · December 2025 · 5 min read

What Epoxy Floor Coating Actually Is

Epoxy is a two-part coating — a resin and a hardener — that chemically bonds to the concrete when mixed and applied. It creates a surface that is significantly harder and more resistant to chemicals, abrasion, and staining than bare concrete. The finished look can range from a clear satin sheen to a high-gloss colour coat, with or without decorative flake.

It is important to distinguish between true epoxy and the one-part floor paints sold at hardware stores. One-part products are much easier to apply but are essentially just paint — they don't bond at the chemical level and will peel and chip under vehicle traffic and the Ontario freeze-thaw cycle. When we talk about garage floor epoxy, we mean the real thing: a professional-grade two-part product applied over properly prepared concrete.

Preparation Is 80% of the Job

The most critical step in any epoxy floor installation is the surface preparation. Concrete that is not properly prepared will delaminate the coating within a season — sometimes within weeks. The concrete must be mechanically ground or acid-etched to open the pores and create a profile that the epoxy can bond to. Any cracks, spalling, or previous coating must be addressed before the epoxy goes down.

We also test for moisture vapour transmission before coating. In Ontario, ground moisture can migrate upward through a concrete slab — especially in spring — and moisture trapped between the slab and the coating is the number-one reason epoxy fails. If moisture levels are high, we address the issue or recommend a breathable coating instead.

What the Application Process Looks Like

After preparation, the first coat — a penetrating primer or base coat — is applied and allowed to cure. Decorative flake, if chosen, is broadcast into the wet base coat at this stage. Then a clear topcoat is applied, which seals the flake and provides the final surface texture and sheen. The full process typically takes two days: preparation and base coat on day one, topcoat on day two. The floor is light-foot-traffic ready within 24 hours of the topcoat, but vehicle-ready takes 72 hours or more.

The curing timeline matters — driving a vehicle over epoxy that hasn't fully cured can leave tire marks and surface impressions that are difficult to remove. We always give homeowners a clear curing schedule before leaving the job site.

What Epoxy Garage Floors Cost in the GTA

A standard two-car garage epoxy floor in the GTA — including grinding, any crack repair, a base coat with decorative flake, and a clear topcoat — typically runs between $1,800 and $3,500 depending on the size of the slab, its condition, and the product system selected. Single-car garages run $1,200 to $2,000. These are real project numbers, not minimum estimates.

Products vary significantly in quality. Lower-cost bids often use diluted epoxy systems or skip the mechanical grinding step in favour of a quick acid wash, which produces an inferior bond. Always ask what preparation method is being used and whether the product is a true two-part epoxy or a single-component coating.

Is It Worth It?

In my experience, yes. Homeowners who do this project consistently report that it changed how they feel about their garage — from a space they tolerated to one they actually enjoy using. The floor is easier to clean, oil and fluid stains don't penetrate, and the visual transformation is dramatic. It's one of the projects with the most immediate and consistent satisfaction.
