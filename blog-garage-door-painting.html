Garage Door Painting: When It Makes Sense and How It's Done

The garage door is one of the largest visual elements on the front of a house — in many Toronto homes, it takes up more facade area than the front door and windows combined. A fresh paint job on a faded, chalky garage door can change the entire feel of the exterior at a fraction of the cost of replacement.

Jay Gonzalez · February 2025 · 5 min read

Paint vs. Replace: When Does It Make Sense to Paint?

Painting makes sense when the door's mechanical function is fine — it opens and closes smoothly, the springs are in good condition, the panels are structurally intact — but the finish has faded, chalked, or peeled. A door in this condition can typically be painted to look like a new door at roughly 10–15% of the replacement cost.

Painting does not make sense if the door panels are dented, cracked, or structurally compromised, if the door is at the end of its functional life, or if the homeowner wants to significantly change the door's style or material profile. In those cases, the conversation is about replacement first and painting second.

Material Matters — Steel, Wood, and Fibreglass

Steel garage doors are the most common in the GTA. They accept paint well but need specific product selection — the paint must be flexible enough to move with the steel's thermal expansion and contraction through Toronto's dramatic seasonal temperature range. Standard exterior paint without sufficient flexibility will crack along the panel edges within a season or two.

Wood garage doors require the most preparation — sanding, priming, and multiple finish coats — but accept paint beautifully when done correctly. Fibreglass doors can be painted but require a specific primer formulated for fibreglass adhesion. Using the wrong primer on fibreglass is the most common reason fibreglass door paint fails quickly.

The Preparation Process

For a steel door, preparation means pressure washing or hand-washing the surface to remove all dirt, oxidation, and chalking from the old finish. Any areas where paint has peeled need to be sanded back to bare metal and spot-primed before the full paint application. If rust is present — common on older steel doors at the bottom where water sits — rust needs to be treated with a rust converter or sanded back before primer, or it will continue spreading under the new paint.

For painted wood doors, the same scraping, sanding, and priming process applies. For a wood door that has been paint-stripped down to bare wood, a full primer coat across the entire surface is required before any finish paint.

Colour Selection for Garage Doors

The garage door colour should relate to the rest of the home's exterior palette — it doesn't need to match anything exactly, but it should be in conversation with the body colour, trim, and front door. The most common approaches are: matching the body colour of the house (the door disappears into the facade), matching the trim colour (the door reads as a large trim element), or using a contrasting accent colour that works with both.

Black and dark charcoal garage doors have become extremely popular in Toronto over the past few years, and they look exceptional against both brick and lighter-coloured siding. If your home's palette can support a dark garage door, it's a high-impact choice that photographs well and adds genuine curb appeal.

Cost and Timeline

A standard two-car steel garage door paint job in the GTA — cleaning, prep, spot priming, and two finish coats — runs $400–$800 depending on the door's condition and size. The job typically takes one day. A wood door in poor condition requiring significant prep work can run higher. These numbers include labour and materials.
