Residential Interior Painting Prices in Toronto

These are the ranges we work with on a daily basis across Toronto and the GTA — broken down by room type, with the factors that push each price up or down. Use this as a reference before requesting quotes.

Jay Gonzalez · October 26, 2026 · 6 min read

2026 Residential Interior Painting Price Guide

Every number below covers standard prep — filling nail holes, light sanding, masking, two finish coats — unless noted otherwise. Prices assume normal ceiling height (8–9 ft) and surfaces in reasonable condition. What pushes a job toward the high end of each range is explained under each section.

Interior Painting — Typical Price Ranges (Toronto & GTA, 2026)

Single bedroom (interior)$400–$700
Living or dining room$600–$1,200
Kitchen — walls only (no cabinets)$500–$900
Kitchen cabinet refinish (full)$1,800–$4,500
Whole-house interior — 1,500 sq ft$4,500–$9,000
Whole-house interior — 2,500 sq ft$7,500–$14,000
Popcorn ceiling removal (per sq ft)$3–$5/sq ft
Condo or apartment turnover$1,500–$3,500

Bedroom: $400–$700

A standard single bedroom — four walls, one coat of primer where needed, two finish coats — sits in this range. The main variables are room size and ceiling height. A large primary bedroom with vaulted or 10-foot ceilings will land at the top of the range or above it. Surface condition also matters: a room with multiple patch repairs, textured walls, or dark existing paint that needs blocking before the new colour will add labour time.

Trim and doors are typically quoted separately unless specified. If you're painting baseboards, window casings, and the door at the same time, expect to add $150–$300 depending on the amount of trim in the room.

Living Room or Dining Room: $600–$1,200

Open-concept living and dining spaces run larger and often have more complex layouts — accent walls, built-ins, feature walls, or extra windows to mask. Square footage is the primary driver here, followed by how many individual wall surfaces need separate treatment.

Ceiling inclusion is the other big factor. Painting the ceiling in a living room adds meaningful time and typically $200–$400 on top of the wall price, depending on size. If the ceiling hasn't been painted in years and needs two coats, that adds further. We always quote ceiling work separately so clients can see what they're getting and decide accordingly.

Kitchen — Walls Only (No Cabinets): $500–$900

Kitchen wall painting is more labour-intensive than a bedroom of similar size, primarily because of what's in the way. Countertops, appliances, and backsplash edges all need careful masking. Grease buildup above the stove and around the range hood needs TSP cleaning before paint will adhere properly — skipping this step is one of the most common reasons paint fails in kitchens within the first year.

Ceiling height and the amount of tile backsplash to mask around are the main variables. A kitchen with a full subway tile backsplash and a range hood that butts up against the wall takes longer to prep than an open layout.

Kitchen Cabinet Refinish (Full): $1,800–$4,500

Cabinet refinishing is a different category from wall painting — it involves removing all doors and drawer fronts, degreasing and sanding every surface, priming with a bonding primer, and applying two spray-applied finish coats. Doors are taken off-site and sprayed in a controlled environment, then rehung once cured.

The range is wide because the job scales with the number of doors and drawers. A small kitchen with 15 doors will sit near $1,800. A large kitchen with 35+ doors, heavy grease buildup, and existing damage to fill will sit near or above $4,500. The finish product also matters — a two-part water-based urethane topcoat is more durable and more expensive than a standard alkyd finish.

Whole-House Interior — 1,500 sq ft: $4,500–$9,000

A full interior repaint on a 1,500 sq ft home covers all living areas, bedrooms, hallways, and typically includes ceilings. The wide range reflects how much prep work is involved. A home that's been freshly renovated with smooth, primed drywall is far quicker to paint than a 30-year-old home with textured walls, multiple patch repairs, and several colour changes from room to room.

Number of rooms matters because more rooms means more masking, more setup time, and more cutting-in at edges. Ceiling height — whether any rooms have vaulted or cathedral ceilings — adds significant time. Staircase and stairwell painting is often the most labour-intensive part of a whole-house project.

Whole-House Interior — 2,500 sq ft: $7,500–$14,000

At this size, the pricing factors above all compound. Trim and doors become a larger portion of the total — painting all baseboards, window casings, door frames, and interior doors in a larger home adds a full day or more of work. Staircases and multi-floor hallways are often the most time-consuming elements.

The upper end of this range reflects homes with extensive prep requirements, multiple wall colours, high ceilings, and trim painting included throughout. The lower end reflects a straightforward repaint in a well-maintained home where all surfaces are in good condition.

Popcorn Ceiling Removal: $3–$5 per sq ft

Popcorn ceiling removal is priced per square foot because it's entirely a labour calculation — the more ceiling there is, the more time it takes. The process involves wetting the texture to loosen it, scraping it off carefully without gouging the drywall below, letting it dry, then skim-coating and sanding to get a smooth finish before paint.

The two things that push the price toward $5/sq ft or above: whether asbestos testing is required (pre-1980s ceilings often contain asbestos, and the material must be handled and disposed of differently if it does), and the condition of the drywall beneath. Ceilings where the texture was applied directly to paper-faced drywall — without primer first — tend to tear when scraped, requiring more extensive skim-coating afterward.

Condo or Apartment Turnover: $1,500–$3,500

Condo turnover painting is a specific type of project with its own constraints. Building access, elevator booking, hallway protection, and working within a fixed time window are all part of the job in a way they're not in a freehold home.

Unit size and the amount of patching required are the main pricing variables. A small studio or one-bedroom in clean condition lands near $1,500. A larger two- or three-bedroom unit with significant wall damage, scuffs, and holes from mounted fixtures can reach $3,500 or beyond. We include standard patching in all turnover quotes — if the walls need extensive drywall repair, that's assessed separately.

How to Use These Numbers

These ranges are a starting point, not a final price. The only way to get an accurate number for your specific project is an on-site assessment — we look at the actual surfaces, measure what needs to be painted, and account for prep requirements before giving you a written quote.

When comparing quotes from different painters, make sure you're comparing the same scope: how many coats, whether trim is included, whether ceiling painting is in the price, and what happens if additional prep work is discovered once work begins. A quote that looks lower may be leaving out labour or coats that you'll end up needing.
