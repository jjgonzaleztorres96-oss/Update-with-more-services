Interior Paint Colour Trends in Toronto for 2026

I walk through a lot of Toronto homes. Here's what I'm seeing on walls across the city right now — the colours that are moving from social media feeds into real living rooms, bedrooms, and kitchens. Not all of them will last, but some of them are genuinely beautiful for the long term.

Jay Gonzalez · March 2025 · 5 min read

Warm Neutrals Are Replacing Cool Greys

For a decade, cool grey was the dominant neutral in Toronto interiors — greige, fog grey, pale silver. That era is ending. The warm neutrals have taken over: warm whites with yellow or pink undertones, creamy off-whites, warm taupes and greiges that lean toward the red-brown spectrum rather than the blue-grey one. Benjamin Moore White Dove, Sherwin-Williams Accessible Beige, and similar warm neutrals are everywhere right now.

The shift reflects a broader movement toward interiors that feel inviting and organic rather than sleek and cool. It's also a practical response to how these colours live in Toronto's light — warm neutrals tend to look better in the relatively low natural light of most Toronto homes than cool greys, which can read blue or washed-out under north-facing or overcast conditions.

Sage Green Is Still Going Strong

Sage green has been the most requested single colour on our projects for the past two years, and it's showing no signs of fading. The appeal is clear — it's a colour that connects the interior to nature, works beautifully with warm wood floors and white trim, and photographs well for anyone who shares their home on social media. The best sage greens have enough grey in them to read sophisticated rather than folksy: Benjamin Moore Pale Sage, Sherwin-Williams Clary Sage, and Farrow and Ball's Mizzle are the ones I see most often.

The trend is moving toward slightly warmer and more muted versions of sage green — olive-leaning rather than blue-leaning — which is good news for longevity. The blue-leaning sages from a few years ago are already starting to feel dated.

Deep, Saturated Colours in Specific Rooms

Bold, saturated colours are being used more frequently — but typically in single rooms rather than throughout an entire home. A deep navy library, a forest green dining room, a terracotta home office. These are rooms with specific character and specific use patterns where a moody, enveloping colour is genuinely appropriate. The rest of the home typically stays quieter.

The most interesting moves I've seen in 2025-2026 involve taking a dark colour and using it on all surfaces in the room — walls, ceiling, and trim — for a fully immersive effect. This tonal dark room approach, when done with quality paint and confident product selection, is one of the most sophisticated interior design moves available.

Warm Whites for Trim and Ceilings

Pure whites — the bright, cold, paper-white trims that were dominant during the grey era — are being replaced by warmer whites on trim, doors, and ceilings. Benjamin Moore White Dove, Chantilly Lace, and Sherwin-Williams Alabaster are the trim whites I'm seeing most frequently specified right now, particularly in homes that are moving toward the warm neutral wall colour palette described above.

The combination of warm wall neutrals with warm white trim creates a more cohesive and less contrasted interior than the bright white trim and cool grey walls combination that defined the previous decade's aesthetic.

One Practical Note on Trends

Paint colour trends are worth knowing, but they're not worth following uncritically. A colour that photographs beautifully in a Toronto design blog may not work in your specific rooms with your specific light and your specific furniture. The best colour is always the one that lives well in your home for the next several years, not the one that was featured in April's issue of a design magazine.
